Today I gave the APEC seminar at [Kavli IPMU](https://research.ipmu.jp/seminar/?seminar_id=3662) on our recent paper with S. Li, [_Self-Interaction Bounds on Ultralight Dark Matter Couplings to Matter_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03477). Here is the idea in a nutshell.

Ultralight dark matter (ULDM) is a framework where particle physics, astrophysics and cosmology meet. Its couplings to Standard Model particles and its self-interactions are usually treated as two separate things, each with its own searches and bounds.

But they are not independent. Any coupling of ULDM to ordinary matter radiatively induces a self-interaction of the dark matter field. Since cosmological and astrophysical observations constrain ULDM self-interactions very strongly, and robustly, those limits can be turned into indirect constraints on how ULDM talks to the Standard Model.

As a proof of principle, this connection gives:

- strong limits on **linear couplings to neutrinos**, in the parameter space relevant for neutrino-oscillation searches for ULDM;
- limits on **quadratic couplings to electrons and light quarks** that can compete with, or even exceed, those from equivalence-principle tests.

In the talk I also discussed the assumptions behind the argument, possible objections such as radiative stability and naturalness, and how far the bounds can be evaded. The broader lesson is that the extraordinary sensitivity of cosmology to ULDM self-interactions may be a powerful and largely unexplored window onto its couplings to the Standard Model.
