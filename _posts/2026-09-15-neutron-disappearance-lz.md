Last week, while reading papers about the new LZ result, I kept seeing the same number: **248 keV**.

At some point on Thursday, I thought: this number looks very familiar.

I remembered a paper I had read some time ago pointing out that bound neutrons in heavy nuclei typically carry momenta of order 250 MeV. If such a neutron suddenly disappears, that momentum is inherited by the residual xenon nucleus. A quick back-of-the-envelope estimate gives a nuclear recoil of about 250 keV, remarkably close to the 248 keV event reported by LZ.

On Saturday night I emailed Alessandro Strumia with the idea and my calculations. We started exchanging calculations and drafts, and by Monday evening we had most of a paper. 🙂

I'm very happy to share the result:

> **Neutron disappearance and the LZ nuclear recoil event**<br>
> M. Aghaie and A. Strumia, [arXiv:2609.09037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09037)

We explore whether the 248 keV LZ nuclear recoil event could originate from the disappearance of a bound neutron, either through spontaneous neutron decay or through a dark-matter-induced reaction.

A particularly interesting possibility occurs for near-threshold, DM-induced neutron disappearance: the process can be kinematically open in xenon while remaining closed in oxygen, and the nuclear recoil spectrum turns from a broad distribution into characteristic lines.

Whether or not this event ultimately has anything to do with neutron disappearance, this was a fun reminder of why unexpected experimental events are worth staring at carefully, and why sometimes recognizing a familiar number is enough to start a new calculation.

[Read the paper on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09037). I hope you enjoy reading it!
